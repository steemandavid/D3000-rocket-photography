# RLC change request — camera rig (CRIG) interface: TRIG + ARM

**For:** the RLC project (`~/claudecode/projects/ESP32-RocketLaunchController`, `steemandavid/ESP32-RocketLaunchController`)
**From:** the CRIG project (`~/claudecode/projects/D3000-rocket-photography`), spec `camera-rig-functional-spec.md` **v0.18**, §8 and Appendix A (connector updated to 5-pin DIN in v0.18)
**Date:** 2026-11-03 · **Status:** requested, not implemented. No RLC code for this exists yet (no `cam_trig` anywhere in the repo).

## 1. What and why

The CRIG is a Nikon D3000 camera rig that photographs liftoffs. Its handheld CRIG remote sits next to the RLC base at the pad, connected by a short 3-core cable on 5-pin DIN 180° connectors. The base tells it two things through two optocouplers inside the CRIG remote:

- **TRIG**: a steady HIGH level from T−2 s until 3 s after the fire pulse, which makes the camera shoot a burst.
- **ARM**: a heartbeat pulse pattern. 1 Hz means "base connected, not armed" and 10 Hz means "base armed". The CRIG uses the armed state to switch the camera on and wake it several seconds before TRIG. It also accepts TRIG **only** while ARM shows the armed pattern.

Both signals are **outputs only**. The base reads nothing from the CRIG and supplies it no power, and the optocouplers give full galvanic isolation. There are no changes to the radio protocol, the RLC remote, or any fire-path element.

## 2. Pins: GPIO39 (TRIG) and GPIO41 (ARM)

Chosen on 2026-11-03 from the spares listed in RLC spec App. C.1 (38, 39, 41) and cross-checked:

- **Code:** `components/rlc_common/include/pin_config.h` (base section) uses 1, 2, 4–18, 21, 40, 42 and 47, plus 48 (common, the WS2812 strip). Nothing in `components/` or `main/` references 38, 39 or 41.
- **Board (ESP32-S3-DevKitC-1, N16R8):** 39 and 41 are none of the strapping pins (0, 3, 45, 46), USB (19, 20), UART0 (43, 44), flash (26–32) or octal PSRAM (35–37). 39 and 41 are the JTAG pad pins MTCK and MTDI, which are unused because debugging goes over USB-JTAG. They are not strapping pins on the S3.
- **GPIO38 stays spare on purpose.** On a DevKitC-1 **v1.1** it drives the on-board RGB LED. This base is a v1.0 board (on-board LED on GPIO48), but a replacement board could be v1.1.
- **Still to do (CRIG open item O-8):** check on the physical base, by continuity or visual check, that nothing is wired to GPIO39 or GPIO41.

## 4. Firmware changes (base)

| # | Change |
|---|---|
| F-1 | `pin_config.h` (base): `PIN_CAM_TRIG 39`, `PIN_CAM_ARM 41`, `PIN_CAM_TRIG_ACTIVE 1`, `PIN_CAM_ARM_ACTIVE 1` (active HIGH). |
| F-2 | `rlc_config.h`: `CAM_TRIG_LEAD_MS 2000`, `CAM_TRIG_POST_MS 3000`, `CAM_TRIG_MAX_MS 10000`, plus a static assert `CAM_TRIG_LEAD_MS < PRE_FIRE_DELAY_MS`. Also `CAM_ARM_TICK_MS 50`, idle pattern 100 ms on / 900 ms off, armed pattern 50 ms on / 50 ms off. |
| F-3 | New module `rlc_cam_trig.c/.h` with `cam_trig_init()`, `cam_trig_on_prefire_enter()`, `cam_trig_on_firing_enter()`, `cam_trig_on_fire_pulse_end()`, `cam_trig_cancel()` and `cam_arm_set(bool armed)`. TRIG uses `esp_timer` one-shots. `cam_trig_on_firing_enter()` sets a `fired` flag; from then on `cam_trig_cancel()` behaves like `cam_trig_on_fire_pulse_end()` (starts the post timer once, never deasserts early), so every FSM hook can simply call `cam_trig_cancel()`. ARM is generated by **one periodic `esp_timer`** (`CAM_ARM_TICK_MS`): the armed pattern while `armed \|\| trig_asserted`, otherwise the idle pattern. The module calls only `gpio_set_level()`: no blocking calls, no radio, no inputs, and it never touches FSM state. |
| F-4 | FSM hooks are listed in the table below. |
| F-5 | The lead-timer callback asserts TRIG **only if** the FSM is still in PRE_FIRE when the timer expires. |
| F-6 | Hard cap: TRIG is deasserted after `CAM_TRIG_MAX_MS` of continuous assertion, whatever the FSM state. |
| F-7 | `cam_trig_init()` runs early in boot, next to relay init, and drives both pins LOW. The ARM timer starts at the end of init with the idle pattern. GPIO39/41 are the JTAG pad pins MTCK/MTDI and their pre-init state is unconfirmed (T-26 will show it). It is harmless either way: even with an internal weak pull-up (about 45 kΩ), the 10 kΩ pull-down holds the line below about 0.6 V, under the PC817 LED threshold. A boot glitch is also neither a 1 Hz nor a 10 Hz pattern. |
| F-8 | If the ARM timer cannot be created or started, log it and leave ARM LOW. The CRIG then reads "not connected" and ignores TRIG, so the photos are lost but the launch is unaffected. Do **not** make this a base ERROR. |

**FSM hooks (F-4):**

| Transition / event | Call |
|---|---|
| IDLE → ARMED | `cam_arm_set(true)` |
| ARMED → PRE_FIRE | `cam_trig_on_prefire_enter()` (start the 3000 ms lead timer) |
| PRE_FIRE → FIRING | `cam_trig_on_firing_enter()` (set `fired`) |
| `EVT_FIRE_PULSE_DONE`, including a pulse completed under `COMPLETE_PULSE_ON_LINK_LOSS` | `cam_trig_on_fire_pulse_end()` (start the 3000 ms post timer, then deassert) |
| Every other exit from PRE_FIRE or FIRING (abort, cease-fire, link lost, key off, disarm, arm sense lost, continuity lost, dead-man) | `cam_trig_cancel()`: before FIRING it deasserts at once; after FIRING it lets the post timer finish |
| Every transition into IDLE, **LINK_LOST** (`do_enter_link_lost()`) or ERROR (disarm, arm timeout, key off, link lost, abort, POST_FIRE → IDLE) | `cam_arm_set(false)` (plus `cam_trig_cancel()` everywhere except POST_FIRE → IDLE, where the post timer finishes TRIG normally) |
| Disarm, ERROR entry, emergency-safe path | `cam_trig_cancel()` + `cam_arm_set(false)` |

Because the pattern is `armed || trig_asserted`, ARM stays at 10 Hz until TRIG drops, about 1 s after POST_FIRE → IDLE (the cooldown is 2 s, the TRIG post time 3 s). That is intended: the CRIG would otherwise ignore the end of the TRIG window.

## 5. Signal timing (what the CRIG expects)

| RLC base | ARM (pin 3) | TRIG (pin 1) |
|---|---|---|
| Power-on / reset, before init | LOW (pull-down) | LOW (pull-down) |
| IDLE, LINK_LOST, ERROR | 1 Hz: 100 ms HIGH / 900 ms LOW | LOW |
| ARMED, PRE_FIRE, FIRING, POST_FIRE, and while TRIG is HIGH | 10 Hz: 50 ms HIGH / 50 ms LOW | — |
| PRE_FIRE + 3000 ms, still in PRE_FIRE | 10 Hz | **HIGH** |
| Fire pulse end + 3000 ms | 10 Hz, then 1 Hz | LOW |
| Any abort / disarm / ERROR / LINK_LOST / emergency safe **before FIRING** | 1 Hz | LOW immediately |
| Any exit from FIRING (pulse completed on link loss, cease-fire, key off, disarm, arm sense lost, ERROR, emergency safe) | 10 Hz while TRIG is HIGH, then 1 Hz | HIGH until the 3000 ms post timer ends |
| `CAM_TRIG_MAX_MS` (10 s) of continuous HIGH | — | LOW |

How the CRIG remote decodes this:
- **Connected:** rising edges 0.7–1.3 s apart.
- **Armed:** three periods in a row of 70–130 ms.
- **Not connected:** no edge for 2.5 s, whether the line is stuck high or stuck low.

So the period tolerance is ±30 %. The pattern needs no more precision than `esp_timer` gives.

## 6. Safety argument (to merge into the RLC spec)

- **Outputs only:** no CRIG signal can reach the fire path or the FSM.
- **Isolation:** optocouplers, and no power drawn from the base.
- **Timer callbacks only:** the GPIO writes come from `esp_timer` callbacks. The periodic ARM callback is a few instructions and reads only its own flags. The fire pulse runs on its own gptimer and is not affected.
- **Shorts:** limited to about 7 mA per line by the 470 Ω resistors.
- **Failures:** any failure of TRIG or ARM (stuck, open, short) affects only the photos. The CRIG ignores TRIG unless ARM shows the armed pattern.

## 7. Tests to add to the RLC test specification

The CRIG IDs are in brackets. Run them with a logic analyser or scope on pins 1–3, or with the CRIG remote connected.

1. **[T-21]** Full sequence with an inert igniter tester:
   - ARM goes to 10 Hz on ARMED and back to 1 Hz about 1 s after POST_FIRE → IDLE.
   - TRIG goes HIGH at PRE_FIRE + 3000 ± 50 ms and LOW at pulse end + 3000 ± 50 ms.
2. **[T-22]** Abort during PRE_FIRE (release the fire button; key off): TRIG goes LOW within 20 ms, and ARM returns to 1 Hz.
3. **[T-23]** Base powered off during TRIG: both lines go LOW.
4. **[T-24]** PRE_FIRE and fire pulse durations unchanged (± 5 ms) with and without the module active.
5. **[T-25]** Short pin 1 or pin 3 to pin 2 (COM−): about 7 mA, and the base keeps operating normally.
6. **[T-26]** After power-on: no pulses during boot, and 1 Hz within 2 s.
7. **[T-27]** ARM follows arm, disarm, arm timeout, key off and ERROR within 100 ms. *Accepted deviation (RLC FSD §15.6):* a new pattern starts in its LOW phase, so after a disarm the first 1 Hz pulse comes about 950 ms later. The 10 Hz pulses must still stop within 100 ms; the CRIG drops ARMED 300 ms after the last fast pulse.
8. **Regression:** the existing fire-path safety tests.

## 8. Answers to the RLC review (2026-11-03)

1. **Link lost during the fire pulse, pulse allowed to finish:** this is treated as a normal pulse end. The post timer runs and TRIG stays HIGH until pulse end + 3 s. One correction to the suggestion: ARM must **not** drop to 1 Hz while TRIG is still HIGH, because the CRIG only accepts TRIG while ARM shows the armed pattern. ARM stays at 10 Hz until TRIG drops (`armed || trig_asserted`), then goes to 1 Hz in LINK_LOST.
2. **Aborts in the middle of the pulse:** keep shooting (owner's decision). Once FIRING has been entered, TRIG always ends through the post timer (or the 10 s hard cap), whatever happens, because the igniter may already have lit. Aborts **before** FIRING still cut TRIG at once. The new hook `cam_trig_on_firing_enter()` and the `fired` flag implement this, so every FSM path can keep calling `cam_trig_cancel()`.
3. **LINK_LOST:** agreed. Hook `do_enter_link_lost()` with `cam_arm_set(false)` (plus `cam_trig_cancel()`, which after FIRING lets the post timer finish).
4. **Physical checks:**
   - **O-8:** the owner will do the continuity check on GPIO39 and GPIO41.
   - **O-9:** the connector is now a **5-pin DIN 180°** female chassis socket (Gotron AD322), not GX12-4. This changed in CRIG spec v0.18; pinout in §3 above: pin 1 TRIG+, pin 2 COM−, pin 3 ARM+, pins 4/5 not connected. If your copy of this document still says GX12-4, it is the old v0.17 version (it was renamed from `RLC_change_request_CRIG_v0.17.md` to `RLC_change_request_CRIG.md`). The panel location is the owner's choice.
5. **Boot state of GPIO39/41:** harmless either way, see F-7. T-26 confirms it.
